# compress-lib v0.4.0 - Deflate and RFC 7692

**Raw DEFLATE both ways, and the per-message rules WebSocket servers and clients need.** v0.4.0 shipped the encoder and the `message` module that ws-net's permessage-deflate builds on.

## What's in 0.4.0

- `deflate::compress` and `deflate::compress_with`: one call, levels 0 to 9 with zlib's meaning.
- `deflate::Deflater`: the streaming encoder. Push input, pull output, with `Flush::None`, `Sync` (byte-aligned, `00 00 FF FF`), `Full` (sync and forget history), or `Finish`. Window bits 8 to 15: no distance is ever longer, and 8 is a real 256-byte window, not rounded up as zlib does. Each block is written as stored, fixed, or dynamic Huffman, whichever is smallest. `set_level` mid-stream, `set_dictionary`, `reset`, `memory_in_use`.
- `message::Compressor` and `message::Decompressor` (RFC 7692 section 7.2): the sync-flush tail removed and put back, context takeover on or off per direction, window bits 8 to 15, final blocks accepted, and the output budget checked while inflating, so a compressed bomb stops at the budget (`OutputLimit`, WebSocket close code 1009). Without takeover the decompressor keeps no window at all: each message's vector is its own history. Fragmented messages decode frame by frame.
- Sizes measured against zlib (through zlib-rs) and miniz_oxide on 1 MiB of text and JSON: equal or smaller at levels 1, 6, and 9 (for example 295,390 bytes at level 6 on text against zlib's 292,057 and miniz_oxide's 296,356; 377,000 at level 1 against 540,095 and 420,309).
- Tests: round trips at every level, window size, and flush pattern, each stream checked by zlib and miniz_oxide; output independent of how the input was split; RFC 7692 section 7.2.3 vectors; the `roundtrip` and `message` fuzz targets.

## Known gap

Compression speed is below the targets: on the shared development machine level 1 ran at about a third of miniz_oxide's speed, and levels 6 and 9 behind zlib-rs. Tuning is the `0.6` milestone, with hosted-runner numbers.

## What's next

`0.5.0` adds zlib (RFC 1950) and gzip (RFC 1952) framing, format detection, and the `std::io` adapters.
