# compress-lib v0.6.0 - Optimisation

**Measured against miniz_oxide, zlib-rs, and C zlib, and tuned.** v0.6.0 added the competitor benchmarks, ran them on GitHub-hosted Linux, macOS, and Windows runners, and made the encoder and the small-message paths faster. The numbers, the method, and the remaining gap are in `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`.

## Measured (hosted runners, 1 MiB inputs)

- Decompression: 1.07 to 2.09 times miniz_oxide's speed on text, JSON, HTML, and binary inputs on all three platforms, and level with zlib-rs (0.86 to 1.23 times), without `unsafe` code or SIMD intrinsics.
- WebSocket per-message compression (200 JSON messages, no context takeover): 1.66 to 4.61 times miniz_oxide.
- Compression at level 6: 1.47 to 1.74 times miniz_oxide on JSON and HTML, 0.98 to 1.19 times on text and binary; the output is within a few bytes of C zlib's at levels 6 and 9.
- The gap: level 1 compression of compressible input runs at 0.62 to 0.97 times miniz_oxide (its output is smaller on text), and compression at levels 6 and 9 at 0.25 to 0.76 times zlib-rs, which uses SIMD and `unsafe`. Closing it within `forbid(unsafe_code)` needs a different match finder, a 1.x candidate.

## What changed

- The match finders keep their state in locals and compare four bytes at a time; the block writer writes each symbol in one operation: 15 to 30% faster at every level with the same match decisions at levels 2 to 9.
- Level 1 became a single-probe matcher over a 4,096-entry hash table that searches more sparsely through long runs without matches: about 1.4 to 1.8 times miniz_oxide on incompressible input instead of a quarter of it.
- Per-message compression without takeover no longer clears 64 KiB of hash table per message, and vector decoding no longer reserves 64 KiB up front: a 70-byte message decodes in about 2.4 microseconds instead of 7.3.
- A match may now refer to the first byte of a stream.
- `deflate::MemoryConfig` sizes the encoder's hash table and symbol buffer (`Deflater::with_memory`, `MessageConfig::with_memory`).
- `bench-compare/` and the `Bench` workflow.
